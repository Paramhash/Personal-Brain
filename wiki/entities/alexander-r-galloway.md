# Alexander R. Galloway

[[Alexander R. Galloway]] is a prominent scholar and media theorist known for his critical analyses of digital culture, networks, and [[Internet Art]].

In "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023), Galloway's work is referenced in several key areas:
*   **Net.art:** He is noted for his 1999 article "Net.art Year in Review: State of net.art 99," where he proclaimed "Net-dot-art is dead," marking the end of its formative period as an underground movement and its incorporation into large-scale exhibitions.
*   **Black Box:** Galloway's definition of a "[[Black Box (Galloway's concept)]]" as an "opaque technological device for which only the inputs and outputs are known" is used to analyze [[Lin Hsin Hsin]]'s celebratory representation of technological apparatuses in her work, such as the PC mouse, in relation to [[Ocarina (1994)]].
*   **Working "on" vs. "within" the digital:** Galloway's distinction between working "on" the digital (taking the medium as its object, a modernist gesture) and working "within" the digital (attention radiating outward to contexts and environments, a non-modernist gesture) is applied to contrast [[Lin Hsin Hsin]]'s practice with that of [[tsunamii.net]].
