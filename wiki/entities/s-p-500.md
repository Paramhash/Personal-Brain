# S&P 500

## Type
Stock Market Index

## Definition
The S&P 500 (Standard & Poor's 500) is a stock market index that represents the performance of 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States. It is widely regarded as one of the best gauges of large-cap U.S. equities and a proxy for the overall health of the U.S. stock market.

## Context from Source
In "Trend Following Trading under a Regime Switching Model" by Dai, Zhang, and Zhu (2010), historical data of the [[S&P 500]] index (specifically from 1962-2008) was utilized in numerical experiments.
- **Testing Strategy Performance**: The authors applied their proposed [[Trend Following Trading]] strategy to S&P 500 data to assess its practical performance and robustness compared to a buy-and-hold approach.
- **Dividend Consideration**: For the S&P 500, the authors explicitly considered the effect of dividends, using annual dividend data to compensate for raw gains, as the index itself does not include dividends in its computation.
- **Parameter Estimation**: Statistics of bull and bear markets for the S&P 500 were used to determine parameters for the [[Regime Switching Model (Financial)]].
