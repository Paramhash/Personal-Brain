# alpha 3.4 (2002)

[[alpha 3.4 (2002)]] is a web-based installation and performance artwork created by the Singaporean art collective [[tsunamii.net]], comprising [[Charles Lim Yi Yong]], [[Woon Tien Wei]], and [[Melvin Phua]]. It was exhibited at [[Documenta11]] in 2002.

The installation featured cables connecting from the ceiling to a row of four glowing LCD screens laid flat on the floor. These screens displayed [[Internet Protocol (IP)]] addresses, websites, GPS coordinates, and data streams, acting as proxies for a performance unfolding beyond the exhibition site. The artists trekked across Germany from Kassel to a web server in Kiel (600 km away), transmitting locative data. A specially created web browser, *webwalker 2.2*, required physical proximity to a server to access a website. The screens showed the artists' IP address, browser attempts (often 404 errors), their GPS route on a satellite map, and geographical locations of visited websites. Upon reaching Kiel, a live feed of the `documenta.de` server was established.

"[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023) analyzes [[alpha 3.4 (2002)]] as a "contemporary" instance of [[Internet Art]] that works "within" the digital. It critically re-politicizes the material and infrastructural dimensions of the internet, disrupting the notion of a smooth, seamless online experience and challenging the rhetoric of technological progress. The work also functions as an institutional critique, pointing to underlying power structures of international mega-exhibitions and what [[Charles Lim Yi Yong]] termed "cultural colonisation" regarding data consolidation.

## Notes
[[alpha 3.4 (2002)]] is a powerful example of how artists can use digital technologies to expose and critique the hidden infrastructures and political implications of the internet, offering a counter-narrative to techno-utopian visions.
