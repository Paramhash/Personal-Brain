# S&P 500 Index

The **S&P 500 Index** is a stock market index that represents the performance of 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States. It is widely regarded as one of the best gauges of large-cap U.S. equities and a proxy for the overall health of the U.S. stock market.

## Relevance to GEX Analysis

In the context of [[../concepts/gamma-exposure-gex.md|Gamma Exposure (GEX)]] analysis, the S&P 500 Index serves as a critical benchmark. Its aggregate GEX profile is often compared against the combined GEX of its individual constituents to identify [[../concepts/gex-divergence-strategies.md|GEX Divergences]].

*   **Index GEX:** The overall GEX of the S&P 500 reflects the collective hedging activity of options market makers across all index-related options (e.g., SPX, SPY).
*   **Internal GEX:** The aggregate GEX of the 500 individual stocks provides insight into the underlying stability or fragility of the index's components.

Divergences between these two measures can signal periods of market "decoupling," where the headline index performance may mask underlying weaknesses or strengths in its components.

## Key Constituents

The performance and GEX profile of the [[../entities/magnificent-7-stocks.md|Magnificent 7 stocks]] (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, AMZN, GOOGL, TSLA, META) are particularly influential due to their massive market capitalization and weight within the S&P 500. Their individual GEX can significantly impact the overall index GEX.
