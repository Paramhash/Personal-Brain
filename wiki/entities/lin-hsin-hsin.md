# Lin Hsin Hsin

[[Lin Hsin Hsin]] is a Singaporean artist, poet, composer, and IT inventor, recognized for her pioneering work in [[New Media Art]] and [[Internet Art]]. She studied painting under notable Nanyang style figures like Liu Kang and Cheong Soo Pieng, and her paintings are held in institutions such as the National Gallery Singapore. Her background in mathematics and computer science significantly informs her artistic practice, which spans 3D sculptures, musical compositions, and poetry.

In the mid-1990s, Lin Hsin Hsin launched the [[Lin Hsin Hsin Art Museum]], which she claimed to be the world's first virtual museum. This online platform served as a space for her digital art, including works like [[Ocarina (1994)]]. Her approach to digital art is described as "modernist," focusing "on" the digital medium itself and celebrating technological apparatuses.

Her work is discussed in "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023) as a key early instance of internet art in Singapore, often existing outside mainstream art discourse of the 1990s.

## Notes
Lin Hsin Hsin's multifaceted career and early adoption of digital platforms for art-making position her as a crucial figure in the history of new media art in Singapore, despite the initial lack of academic attention to her digital practice.
