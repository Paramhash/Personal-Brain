# Risks for the Long Run: A Potential Resolution of Asset Pricing Puzzles (Bansal & Yaron, 2004)

**Authors:** Ravi Bansal and Amir Yaron
**Publication Year:** 2004
**Journal:** *The Journal of Finance*

## Summary
This influential paper by [[../entities/ravi_bansal.md|Ravi Bansal]] and [[../entities/amir_yaron.md|Amir Yaron]] introduces the "long-run risk" model, which attempts to resolve several asset pricing puzzles, including the equity premium puzzle and the risk-free rate puzzle. The model posits that investors are concerned not only about short-term consumption fluctuations but also about persistent (long-run) changes in consumption growth and economic uncertainty.

## Key Contribution
The [[../concepts/long_run_risk_model.md|long-run risk model]] suggests that small, persistent shocks to expected consumption growth and volatility can have a significant impact on asset prices. Investors demand a higher [[../concepts/equity_risk_premium.md|equity risk premium]] to compensate for exposure to these long-run risks, especially for assets that perform poorly when expected consumption growth declines or volatility increases.

## Implications for Horizon Effects
The model predicts that the [[../concepts/equity_risk_premium.md|equity risk premium]] should generally increase with the investment horizon, as long-run risks become more prominent. This prediction is often contrasted with observed market behavior during crises, where short-term risk premia can exceed long-term premia, leading to a negative [[../concepts/horizon_spread_financial.md|horizon spread]], as discussed in [[../sources/detecting_stock_market_regimes_lai_2022.md|Lai (2022)]].
