# Predicting Excess Stock Returns Out of Sample: Can Anything Beat the Historical Average? (Campbell & Thompson, 2008)

**Authors:** John Y. Campbell and Samuel B. Thompson
**Publication Year:** 2008
**Journal:** *The Review of Financial Studies*

## Summary
This paper by [[../entities/john_y_campbell.md|John Y. Campbell]] and [[../entities/samuel_b_thompson.md|Samuel B. Thompson]] rigorously examines the out-of-sample predictability of excess stock returns using various financial ratios, such as the dividend-price ratio, earnings-price ratio, and book-to-market ratio. They investigate whether these predictors can consistently outperform a simple historical average forecast.

## Key Contribution
The authors find that while many variables show in-sample predictability, their out-of-sample performance is often weak or inconsistent. However, they identify certain variables, particularly the dividend yield, that can offer modest but statistically significant improvements over the historical average, especially when imposing economically sensible restrictions on the coefficients. The paper emphasizes the importance of out-of-sample testing for evaluating forecast performance.

## Impact on Forecasting
This paper established a benchmark for evaluating the predictability of equity returns and highlighted the challenges of outperforming simple models. Its methodology for evaluating out-of-sample forecasts is widely adopted. For example, [[../sources/detecting_stock_market_regimes_lai_2022.md|Lai (2022)]] builds on this benchmark model to compare the forecasting performance of different [[../concepts/regime_switching_models.md|regime switching models]] for the [[../concepts/equity_risk_premium.md|equity risk premium]], using the dividend yield as a predictor.
