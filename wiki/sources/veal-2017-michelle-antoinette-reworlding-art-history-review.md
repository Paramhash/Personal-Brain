# Michelle Antoinette, Reworlding Art History: Contemporary Southeast Asian Art after 1990 (Review)

**Author:** Clare Veal
**Publication:** *Southeast of Now: Directions in Contemporary and Modern Art in Asia*, Volume 1, Number 2, October 2017, pp. 181-188
**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1353/sen.2017.0019](https://doi.org/10.1353/sen.2017.0019)

## Summary
Clare Veal's article is a review of Michelle Antoinette's book, *Reworlding Art History: Contemporary Southeast Asian Art after 1990*. Veal highlights Antoinette's central argument that contemporary art's heterogeneity challenges traditional art historical frameworks, and that Southeast Asia serves as a "paradigmatic example" of how "reworlding" has disrupted Euro-American-centric art narratives.

The review discusses Antoinette's definition of "contemporary" art in Southeast Asia, which rejects strict periodization and frames the 1990s as a watershed moment due to institutionalization and commercialization. Veal notes Antoinette's focus on "reworlding" as a means to understand the acceptance of Southeast Asian art by international audiences, implying a formulation of the world from non-Euro-American perspectives.

Veal outlines the book's structure:
1.  **"Preliminary Encounters"**: Reviews current approaches to contemporary Southeast Asian art, identifying the representation of "difference" in a global context as a central problematic. Antoinette critiques the commodification of "the other" and excessive dependence on culturalist discourses, advocating for a methodology that considers both similarity and difference.
2.  **"Locating Southeast Asian Difference"**: Addresses the formation of Southeast Asian geographies as interpretive frameworks, contrasting state-driven art diplomacy with independent arts organizations. Veal suggests the book could have benefited from discussing pre-1990s cartographies and including artists from mainland Southeast Asia to explore "limits of contemporaneity" in isolated contexts.
3.  **"Counterpoints: Southeast Asia in Practice"**: Focuses on the affective and formal qualities of works as a counter-approach to reductive ethnographic readings. This section, according to Veal, makes the most compelling case for using Southeast Asia as a critical framework.

Veal praises Antoinette's compelling analyses of individual artworks, such as [[lani-maestro]]'s *a book thick of ocean* (1993) and [[yee-i-lann]]'s *Sulu Stories* (2005), and [[simryn-gill]]'s *Self-seeds* (1998), which effectively illustrate the dynamic nature of artistic subjectivities and cultural identity as a process. While acknowledging the book's contribution to understanding Southeast Asian art and the broader concept of "reworlding," Veal notes some repetition and theoretical discussions that can be less engaging than the artwork analyses.

## Key Takeaways
*   Antoinette's *Reworlding Art History* posits Southeast Asia as crucial for understanding how contemporary art challenges Euro-American dominance.
*   "Reworlding" involves shifting perspectives from Euro-American to non-Euro-American models of art history and world-making.
*   The book critiques the commodification of "difference" and culturalist interpretations of Southeast Asian art.
*   Individual artwork analyses are a strength, demonstrating how art can explore identity as a process and negotiate global/local forces.
