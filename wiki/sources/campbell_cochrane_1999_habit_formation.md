# By Force of Habit: A Consumption-Based Explanation of Aggregate Stock Market Behavior (Campbell & Cochrane, 1999)

**Authors:** John Y. Campbell and John H. Cochrane
**Publication Year:** 1999
**Journal:** *Journal of Political Economy*

## Summary
This influential paper by [[../entities/john_y_campbell.md|John Y. Campbell]] and [[../entities/john_h_cochrane.md|John H. Cochrane]] proposes a [[../concepts/consumption_based_asset_pricing.md|consumption-based asset pricing model]] that incorporates "habit formation." In this model, an investor's utility depends not just on the absolute level of consumption, but on consumption relative to a slowly moving external habit level.

## Key Contribution
The [[../concepts/habit_formation_model.md|habit formation model]] explains several empirical puzzles in asset markets, including the [[../concepts/equity_premium_puzzle.md|equity premium puzzle]] and the [[../concepts/risk_free_rate_puzzle.md|risk-free rate puzzle]], as well as the predictability of stock returns. When consumption is close to the habit level (e.g., during recessions), investors become highly risk-averse, demanding a large [[../concepts/equity_risk_premium.md|equity risk premium]]. When consumption is far above the habit level (e.g., during booms), investors are less risk-averse, and the equity premium is lower.

## Implications for Horizon Effects
Similar to the [[../concepts/long_run_risk_model.md|long-run risk model]], the [[../concepts/habit_formation_model.md|habit formation model]] generally predicts that investors should require a higher long-term [[../concepts/equity_risk_premium.md|equity risk premium]] to compensate for long-run risks. This theoretical prediction is often compared with empirical observations, particularly the reversal of the [[../concepts/horizon_spread_financial.md|horizon spread]] during crisis periods, as discussed in [[../sources/detecting_stock_market_regimes_lai_2022.md|Lai (2022)]].
