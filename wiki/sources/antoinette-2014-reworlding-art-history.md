# Reworlding Art History: Contemporary Southeast Asian Art after 1990

**Author:** Michelle Antoinette
**Publication:** Amsterdam: Brill, 2014

## Summary
Michelle Antoinette's *Reworlding Art History: Contemporary Southeast Asian Art after 1990* argues that the heterogeneity of contemporary art challenges singular aesthetic, ideological, political, geographical, or social frameworks. The book posits Southeast Asia as a "paradigmatic example" of how a "reworlding" of contemporary art has challenged the Euro-American-centric nature of art markets, worlds, and histories.

Antoinette defines "contemporary" art in Southeast Asia not by strict periodization, but by its co-existence with "modern" art, while framing the 1990s as a crucial period for its advent due to institutionalization, commercialization, and global recognition of "Other" and "peripheral" art worlds.

The core concept of "reworlding" implies formulating the world from a particular perspective, specifically shifting from Euro-American models to non-Euro-American perspectives. It serves as a critical tactic to disrupt binary formations (e.g., global vs. local, Western vs. non-Western) and homogenizing narratives, both Euro-American and those within Southeast Asia itself.

The book is structured into three parts:
1.  **"Preliminary Encounters"**: Reviews existing literature on contemporary art, Southeast Asia, and its art histories, identifying the representation of "difference" in a global context as a central issue. Antoinette critiques the tendency of international exhibitions to commodify the "exotic" and the over-reliance on culturalist interpretations, advocating for an approach that considers both similarity and difference.
2.  **"Locating Southeast Asian Difference"**: Examines how Southeast Asian geographies are framed as interpretive exhibitory frameworks, contrasting state-driven diplomatic uses of art with grassroots initiatives.
3.  **"Counterpoints: Southeast Asia in Practice"**: Focuses on the affective and formal qualities of artworks as a means to move beyond reductive ethnographic or politically-focused readings. This section includes discussions of artists like [[simryn-gill]], [[lani-maestro]], and [[yee-i-lann]], demonstrating how their works negotiate localization and globalization, and explore identity as a process.

## Key Arguments
*   Contemporary Southeast Asian art after 1990 is a crucial site for understanding the "reworlding" of art history.
*   "Reworlding" involves de-centering Euro-American perspectives and acknowledging diverse "worldings" of art.
*   The book advocates for an art historical methodology that embraces both the specificities and connections of contemporary Southeast Asian art, moving beyond reductive identity politics or culturalist interpretations.
*   Artists in Southeast Asia actively engage with and negotiate processes of localization and globalization.
