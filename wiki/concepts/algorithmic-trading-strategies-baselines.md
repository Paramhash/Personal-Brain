# Algorithmic Trading Strategies (Baselines)

Algorithmic trading strategies are automated systems that execute trades based on predefined rules and mathematical models. When evaluating new, advanced trading frameworks like [[../entities/tradingagents-framework.md|TradingAgents]], it is common practice to benchmark their performance against established, simpler baseline strategies. These baselines provide a reference point to determine if the new approach offers significant improvements.

Here are some common baseline strategies used in financial trading research:

## 1. Buy and Hold

*   **Description:** A passive investment strategy where an investor buys a set of assets (e.g., stocks) and holds them for the entire investment period, regardless of market fluctuations.
*   **Rationale:** Assumes that markets tend to rise over the long term, and attempts to time the market are often unsuccessful.
*   **Use as Baseline:** Represents the return achievable without any active trading decisions, serving as a fundamental benchmark for any active strategy.

## 2. MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)

*   **Description:** A trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two moving averages of a security’s price. It generates buy and sell signals based on crossovers between the MACD line and a signal line (typically a 9-period EMA of the MACD line).
*   **Signals:**
    *   **Buy:** When the MACD line crosses above the signal line.
    *   **Sell:** When the MACD line crosses below the signal line.
*   **Use as Baseline:** A widely used and relatively simple technical analysis strategy.

## 3. KDJ and RSI (Relative Strength Index)

*   **Description:** A momentum strategy that combines two popular technical indicators:
    *   **KDJ (Stochastic Oscillator):** Identifies overbought and oversold conditions by comparing a security's closing price to its price range over a given period.
    *   **RSI (Relative Strength Index):** Measures the speed and change of price movements, also indicating overbought (typically >70) or oversold (typically <30) conditions.
*   **Signals:** Generated by identifying extreme conditions or crossovers within these indicators.
*   **Use as Baseline:** Represents a combination of momentum-based technical indicators.

## 4. ZMR (Zero Mean Reversion)

*   **Description:** A mean reversion trading strategy that assumes asset prices will revert to their historical average or "mean" over time. It generates signals based on price deviations from, and subsequent reversions to, a zero reference line (often a detrended price series).
*   **Signals:**
    *   **Buy:** When prices are significantly below the mean (oversold).
    *   **Sell:** When prices are significantly above the mean (overbought).
*   **Use as Baseline:** Represents a common class of strategies that profit from temporary price deviations.

## 5. SMA (Simple Moving Average)

*   **Description:** A trend-following strategy that generates trading signals based on crossovers between short-term and long-term simple moving averages (SMAs) of an asset's price.
*   **Signals:**
    *   **Buy:** When the short-term SMA crosses above the long-term SMA (golden cross).
    *   **Sell:** When the short-term SMA crosses below the long-term SMA (death cross).
*   **Use as Baseline:** Another fundamental and widely used technical analysis strategy for trend identification.

These baseline strategies are essential for providing context to the performance of more complex [[../concepts/llms-in-financial-trading.md|LLM-driven trading systems]], allowing researchers to quantify the value added by advanced AI techniques.
