# Internet Art

[[Internet Art]], also known as net.art, refers to artistic projects and gestures that "happen" and take place through the networked properties of the internet. It is considered a subset of [[New Media Art]].

Historically, [[Internet Art]] gained prominence in the 1990s, particularly with the Eastern European and Russian net.art movement. The adoption of "postinternet art" in the 2010s signaled a shift, recognizing the internet as a mass medium with deeper cultural, corporate, and political complexities. Despite its early emergence, internet art has often had an "uneven and conflicted relationship with the established art world" and has been largely marginal within wider accounts of art history.

In "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023), two early instances of [[Internet Art]] from Singapore are analyzed:
*   [[Ocarina (1994)]] by [[Lin Hsin Hsin]], housed in the virtual [[Lin Hsin Hsin Art Museum]], representing a "modernist" engagement with the digital medium itself.
*   [[alpha 3.4 (2002)]] by the collective [[tsunamii.net]], exhibited at [[Documenta11]], which critically engages with the material and infrastructural dimensions of the internet.

The article highlights the challenges of historicizing [[Internet Art]] in Singapore, where it faced "strategic amnesia" and was often perceived as a novelty, separate from dominant art discourses.

## Notes
[[Internet Art]] represents a crucial phase in the evolution of digital art, challenging traditional art institutions and expanding the definition of artistic practice through networked technologies. Its history, particularly in non-Western contexts, often requires a media archaeological approach to uncover its significance.
