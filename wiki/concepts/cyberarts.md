# Cyberarts

[[Cyberarts]] is a term specifically deployed by [[Gunalan Nadarajan]] in the early 2000s to describe art practices in Singapore that contended with the "cyberneticisation of society." Drawing cues from [[Norbert Wiener]]'s 1948 conception of cybernetics as the science of control and communications in human and nonhuman systems, Nadarajan saw [[Cyberarts]] as an ideal term for art engaging with increasingly complex technological systems managing information flows.

The [[Cyberarts]] movement in 2000s Singapore manifested through exhibitions, residencies, new artworks, and educational programs. A key event was the *Cyberarts: Intersections of Art and Technology* exhibition in 2001, curated by Nadarajan, which featured [[Internet Art]], game art, and multimedia installations. This period also saw the launch of the National Arts Council's New Media Arts Fund and the establishment of the Cyberarts/Cyberculture Research Initiative (CCRI) at NUS.

In "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023), [[Cyberarts]] is presented as a facet of broader [[New Media Art]] practices, highlighting a moment of concerted institutional support for technology-engaged art in Singapore.
