# Contemporary Southeast Asian Art after 1990

## Overview
The period of contemporary Southeast Asian art after 1990 is identified as a significant era characterized by its heterogeneity and resistance to singular aesthetic, ideological, political, geographical, or social frameworks. This period is central to Michelle Antoinette's concept of "reworlding art history," as it exemplifies how non-Euro-American art challenges established art historical narratives.

## Key Characteristics and Developments
*   **Watershed Moment (1990s)**: The 1990s are considered a crucial period due to the "gathering density" of contemporary Southeast Asian art, confirmed by its parallel institutionalization (museums, exhibitions, scholarship) and commercialization (art markets, private collectors). This era also saw external (Western) art curators and collectors increasingly engaging directly with artists in the region.
*   **Rejection of Strict Periodization**: Contemporary art in Southeast Asia is not strictly separated from "modern" art; rather, modern art practices may coexist alongside contemporary ones.
*   **Challenge to Euro-American Centricity**: The art of this period actively challenges the Euramerican-centric nature of art markets, worlds, and histories, contributing to a "reworlding" of global art narratives.
*   **"Difference" in a Global Context**: A central problematic is the representation of "difference" in a global context. Artists and scholars aim to acknowledge the specificity of local contexts while resisting the reduction of artistic practice to identity politics or the commodification of "the other."
*   **Beyond Culturalist Discourses**: There is a critique of "excessive dependence on culturalist discourses" that ethnically map or typecast Southeast Asian art. Instead, an emphasis is placed on understanding art through its affective and formal qualities, and its ability to explore universal human experiences.
*   **Identity as Process**: Artworks often explore cultural identity not as a fixed entity but as something "in process and production," reflecting the dynamic and complex nature of artistic subjectivities in the region.
*   **Negotiation of Global and Local**: Artists engage with and negotiate the forces of localization and globalization, often through themes of international mobility, cosmopolitanisms, and the rewriting of memory and history.
