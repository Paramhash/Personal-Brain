# Razor-and-Blade Business Model

The razor-and-blade business model is a strategy where a core product (the "razor") is sold at a low price or even at a loss, to increase sales of a complementary, high-margin consumable product (the "blades"). This model aims to generate long-term, recurring revenue streams.

## Application in Medical Technology
This model is highly prevalent and effective in the medical technology sector, particularly in [[../concepts/surgical-robotics.md|surgical robotics]]:
*   **[[../entities/intuitive-surgical.md|Intuitive Surgical]]**: A prime example, selling its *[[../entities/da-vinci-surgical-system.md|da Vinci]]* robotic systems (the "razor") and generating substantial, high-margin recurring revenue from proprietary instruments, accessories, and software service agreements (the "blades"). This contributes to its elite [[../concepts/operating-margin.md|operating margins]].
*   **[[../entities/medtronic.md|Medtronic]]'s Strategy**: Medtronic's strategic focus is to convert its robotic and cardiovascular sales from one-time hardware placements into highly lucrative, sticky, recurring revenue streams through proprietary consumables, instruments, and software service agreements, effectively adopting this model to expand its margins.
*   **[[../entities/johnson-and-johnson.md|Johnson & Johnson]]**: Also employs this strategy by absorbing lower initial margins on robotic hardware placements, knowing it will capture high-margin recurring revenue on instruments and consumables later.
