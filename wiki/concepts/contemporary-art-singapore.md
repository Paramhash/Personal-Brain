# Contemporary Art in Singapore

[[Contemporary Art in Singapore]] is a complex and evolving field, particularly when considering its relationship with [[New Media Art]] and technology. The discourse around it often grapples with the fundamental question of "the modern" and how to chart its trajectory beyond a predominantly Western framework.

"[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023) highlights the challenges in historicizing [[New Media Art]] within [[Contemporary Art in Singapore]]. Early internet art practices, such as those by [[Lin Hsin Hsin]] in the 1990s, largely escaped art historical attention, seen as anomalous against efforts to consolidate a "modern" and "contemporary" aesthetic specific to Singapore. Technology was often perceived as an alienating (Western) factor.

[[Gunalan Nadarajan]]'s essay "Not Modern: Theses on Contemporary Art" emphasizes the notion of heterogeneity as crucial for critical inquiry into [[Contemporary Art in Singapore]]. He argues against viewing contemporary art as a direct successor to modern art, advocating for a constellation of heterogeneous histories. The article suggests that to understand [[Contemporary Art in Singapore]], one must delve into existing technological and art histories, acknowledging the influence of colonial technology and the complexities of defining modernity in a non-Western context.

## Notes
The discussion of [[Contemporary Art in Singapore]] reveals the ongoing struggle to integrate diverse artistic practices, particularly those involving technology, into a coherent national art historical narrative, often complicated by post-colonial contexts and the perceived "newness" of digital media.
