# Media Archaeology

[[Media Archaeology]] is a theoretical framework that investigates [[New Media Art]] cultures by drawing insights from past media. As elucidated by [[Jussi Parikka]], it places particular emphasis on "the forgotten, the quirky, the non-obvious apparatuses, practices and interventions."

Rather than solely writing historical narratives, media archaeology seeks to study themes and ideas from past media to understand the meanings of "newness" in contemporary media. It aims to disrupt treatments of new media as an "all-encompassing and 'timeless' realm that can be explained from within."

In the context of "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023), media archaeology is employed to challenge the [[Strategic Amnesia of Digital Culture]] surrounding new media art in Singapore. Key themes of media archaeology, such as modernity, histories of the present, and alternative histories, guide the analysis of early [[Internet Art]] works like [[Ocarina (1994)]] by [[Lin Hsin Hsin]] and [[alpha 3.4 (2002)]] by [[tsunamii.net]].

## Notes
[[Media Archaeology]] offers a crucial lens for understanding the complex and often overlooked histories of digital art, particularly in contexts where narratives of "newness" tend to dominate.
