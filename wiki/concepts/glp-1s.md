# GLP-1s (Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists)

GLP-1s are a class of pharmacological therapies primarily used for the management of Type 2 diabetes and, increasingly, for weight management. These drugs mimic the action of the natural GLP-1 hormone, which helps regulate blood sugar and appetite.

The rise of GLP-1s has significantly impacted the [[../concepts/medical-device-response-to-glp-1s.md|medical device industry]], prompting a strategic shift from a defensive posture to one of integration and adaptation. While initially perceived as a threat to devices, GLP-1s are now often viewed as complementary, driving new opportunities for device makers in areas like metabolic monitoring, patient identification, and high-acuity interventions.

Key impacts on the medical device sector include:
*   Driving the need for [[../concepts/continuous-glucose-monitors-cgms.md|Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGMs)]] to track metabolic health and drug efficacy.
*   Influencing the development of [[../concepts/automated-insulin-delivery-aid-systems.md|Automated Insulin Delivery (AID) systems]] for Type 2 diabetics.
*   Creating [[../concepts/expanded-patient-funnels-medical-devices.md|expanded patient funnels]] for conditions like sleep apnea and orthopedics.
*   Shifting focus towards [[../concepts/high-acuity-innovation-medical-devices.md|high-acuity innovation]] where pharmacological therapy is insufficient.

GLP-1s are considered a significant factor in the evolution of [[../concepts/digital-health-ecosystems.md|digital health ecosystems]], especially when paired with data-driven hardware.
