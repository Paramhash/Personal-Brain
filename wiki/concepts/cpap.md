# CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure)

CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure) is a common treatment for sleep apnea, a condition where breathing repeatedly stops and starts during sleep. A CPAP machine delivers a continuous stream of air through a mask worn during sleep, keeping the airway open.

Initially, the rise of [[../concepts/glp-1s.md|GLP-1]] therapies raised concerns for companies like [[../entities/resmed.md|ResMed]], as weight loss could potentially reverse weight-induced sleep apnea. However, data suggests that GLP-1s are actually contributing to [[../concepts/expanded-patient-funnels-medical-devices.md|expanded patient funnels]] for CPAP devices.

Patients on GLP-1s have shown higher CPAP adherence rates. Furthermore, the increased number of obese individuals visiting doctors for weight-loss prescriptions are being identified and diagnosed with sleep apnea, creating a previously "hidden" patient population for CPAP devices. This demonstrates how device makers are adapting to GLP-1s by leveraging the "discovery effect" to expand their market.
