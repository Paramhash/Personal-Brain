# Obtaining Hidden Markov Model (HMM) Estimates from Option Prices

Obtaining [Hidden Markov Model](../concepts/hidden-markov-model.md) (HMM) state probabilities directly from option prices is a sophisticated workflow that leverages the forward-looking nature of options. Unlike using backward-looking stock returns, option prices reflect market expectations, yielding **Implied Risk-Neutral State Probabilities** ($\mathbb{Q}$-measures) rather than real-world physical probabilities ($\mathbb{P}$-measures). This distinction is crucial for understanding market pricing of future events, as detailed in [Risk-Neutral vs. Real-World Probabilities](../concepts/risk-neutral-vs-real-world-probabilities.md).

For short-dated options traders (e.g., 1DTE to 7DTE), this approach provides insights into the immediate future market regime priced in by participants. It's important to note that raw option prices cannot be directly fed into standard HMM libraries like [hmmlearn](../entities/hmmlearn.md); instead, they must first be transformed into a continuous surface of mathematical features or used within a calibration engine.

There are two primary methods to achieve this:

## Method 1: The Non-Parametric Surface Clustering Approach

This method is best suited for speed, execution, and machine learning backtesters. It involves extracting descriptive features from the [Implied Volatility Surface (IVS)](../concepts/implied-volatility-surface.md) and treating these features as the observable emissions ($O_t$) of the HMM.

### Step 1: Feature Extraction from the Option Chain

For a target expiration, three specific metrics are extracted from the volatility smile:

1.  **[ATM Volatility](../concepts/atm-volatility.md):** The implied volatility of the at-the-money straddle, measuring overall market fear.
2.  **[Skew (Options)](../concepts/options-skew.md):** The difference between Out-of-the-Money (OTM) Put IV and OTM Call IV (e.g., 25-Delta Put IV minus 25-Delta Call IV), measuring asymmetric tail-risk pricing.
3.  **[Smile Curvature](../concepts/smile-curvature.md):** The average of OTM Put and Call IVs minus the ATM IV, measuring the market's expected kurtosis or jump risk.

### Step 2: Feed into a Continuous Gaussian HMM

These three features are compiled into a daily sequence vector $X_t = [\text{ATM}_t, \text{Skew}_t, \text{Curvature}_t]$ and then used to fit a [Gaussian HMM](../concepts/gaussian-hmm.md). This HMM identifies underlying "mood shifts" of option market makers rather than tracking price changes.

```python
import numpy as np
from hmmlearn.hmm import GaussianHMM

# X is an (N_days, 3) matrix containing [ATM_Vol, Put_Skew, Smile_Curvature]
model = GaussianHMM(n_components=3, covariance_type="full", n_iter=100)
model.fit(X)

# Extract the exact probability distribution for today's state
# This returns an array of shape (3,) e.g., [0.85, 0.12, 0.03]
current_state_probabilities = model.predict_proba(X)[-1]
```

The output probabilities across hidden states can be interpreted as structural market dynamics, such as a calm bull market, indiscriminate selling panic, or a squeezing bull market/aggressive gamma trap.

## Method 2: Parametric Structural Calibration ($\mathbb{Q}$-Measure)

This method is ideal for highly precise mathematical pricing models and institutional risk managers, aiming to determine the mathematically exact risk-neutral probability of market state switches. It bypasses historical data by inverting a [Regime-Switching Option Pricing Model](../concepts/regime-switching-option-pricing-model.md).

In contrast to the standard [Black-Scholes Model](../concepts/black-scholes-model.md) where volatility is constant, a Regime-Switching Black-Scholes Model assumes the asset's volatility ($\sigma_t$) instantly jumps between discrete hidden states (e.g., $\sigma_{\text{Low}} = 12\%$ and $\sigma_{\text{High}} = 35\%$), governed by a [transition matrix](../concepts/transition-matrix.md). The underlying asset movement is still modeled via [Geometric Brownian Motion](../concepts/geometric-brownian-motion.md).

### The Objective Function Loop

To obtain current probabilities from today's market option prices, a global [optimization routine](../concepts/optimization-algorithms-in-finance.md) (such as Nelder-Mead or Differential Evolution) is used to calibrate the hidden parameters. The objective is to minimize the [Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)](../concepts/root-mean-squared-error.md) between theoretical prices generated by the HMM pricing model ($C^{\text{HMM}}$) and actual market quotes ($C^{\text{Market}}$) across various strikes and expiries.

$$\min_{\Pi, \mathbf{P}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}} \sum_{k=1}^{M} \left( C_{k}^{\text{HMM}}(\Pi, \mathbf{P}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}) - C_{k}^{\text{Market}} \right)^2$$

The parameters solved for include:
*   $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [\sigma_1, \sigma_2]$: The baseline volatility of each hidden regime.
*   $\mathbf{P} = \begin{pmatrix} p_{00} & p_{01} \\ p_{10} & p_{11} \end{pmatrix}$: The risk-neutral transition probability matrix.
*   $\Pi = [\pi_1, \pi_2]$: The current latent state probabilities.

### Execution Tip for Traders

Since this optimization uses *only today's snapshot* of the options chain, the resulting $\Pi$ vector provides an immediate, forward-looking market consensus. Discrepancies between this method's output and historical, return-based HMMs can signal institutional positioning (e.g., aggressive tail-risk buying) before it manifests in the underlying asset.
