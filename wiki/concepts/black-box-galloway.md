# Black Box (Galloway's concept)

The "[[Black Box (Galloway's concept)]]" is a concept defined by [[Alexander R. Galloway]] as "an opaque technological device for which only the inputs and outputs are known." This term highlights the inherent illegibility of a technology's internal workings, despite its functional exterior.

In "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023), Galloway's concept of the [[Black Box (Galloway's concept)]] is applied to analyze the representation of technological apparatuses in [[Lin Hsin Hsin]]'s work, particularly in relation to [[Ocarina (1994)]]. The article notes how the computer mouse, described as functioning like "magic," exemplifies this concept. Its illegibility, despite its functional exterior, reinforces the apparent "magic" of its function, housing a "mystical kernel" within a "rational shell."

This celebratory frame through which technology is represented in [[Lin Hsin Hsin]]'s [[Lin Hsin Hsin Art Museum]] produces interesting slippages in time, maintaining digital aesthetics from an earlier timeframe while celebrating more recent technologies.

## Notes
The [[Black Box (Galloway's concept)]] is a powerful analytical tool for understanding how users and artists perceive and interact with technology, often focusing on its apparent functionality rather than its complex internal mechanisms or historical development.
