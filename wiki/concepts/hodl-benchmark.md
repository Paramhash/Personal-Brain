# HODL Benchmark
**In one line:** A passive investment strategy where the initial inventory of assets is simply held (HODL) without any active management, used as a baseline for performance comparison.
## Intuition
For an active market-making strategy, it's not enough to just make a profit; it must also demonstrate that it adds value beyond what a simple, passive holding strategy would achieve. The [[hodl-benchmark]] provides this baseline, helping to determine if the active strategy generates "alpha" or merely captures market returns.
## Mechanism / math
The bot's final equity ($E_{close}$) is compared against the equity that would have resulted from holding the initial inventory ($E_{HODL\ benchmark}$).
The [[cl-success-metrics]] include this as a secondary objective:
$$
E_{close} > E_{HODL\ benchmark}
$$
If the bot's equity is higher than at entry but lower than the HODL benchmark, it made an absolute profit but underperformed the alternative.
## Where it matters
The [[hodl-benchmark]] is a key component of the [[cl-success-metrics]] for evaluating the true value added by an active [[cl-market-making]] strategy. It helps to differentiate between profits derived from market exposure (which HODL would also capture) and profits generated by the market-making activity itself.
## Evidence and limits
The [[warehouse-manager-cl-inventory-strategy]] includes the [[hodl-benchmark]] as a "Secondary objective" for success, stating that if the bot makes an absolute profit but underperforms HODL, it indicates a less optimal strategy. This highlights its role in assessing relative performance.
