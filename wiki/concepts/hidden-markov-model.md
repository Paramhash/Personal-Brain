# Hidden Markov Model (HMM)

A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) is a statistical Markov model in which the system being modeled is assumed to be a Markov process with unobserved (hidden) states. It is a generative probabilistic model that describes a sequence of observable events (features) that depend on a sequence of internal states that are not directly observable.

Key components of an HMM include:
*   **Hidden States (S):** The unobservable states of the system.
*   **Observations (O):** The observable events that are generated by the hidden states.
*   **Transition Probabilities (A):** The probabilities of moving from one hidden state to another.
*   **Emission Probabilities (B):** The probabilities of observing a particular event given a hidden state.
*   **Initial State Probabilities (π):** The probabilities of starting in each hidden state.

HMMs are widely used in various fields, including speech recognition, bioinformatics, and financial modeling. In the context of [[../concepts/near-expiry-hmm-options-dynamics.md|Near-Expiry HMM for SPX/SPY Options Dynamics]], HMMs are used to infer latent market microstructure regimes (e.g., [[../concepts/market-regime-pinning.md|pinning]], [[../concepts/market-regime-mean-reverting.md|mean-reverting]], [[../concepts/market-regime-gamma-squeeze.md|gamma-squeeze]]) from observable options market features.

Common algorithms associated with HMMs include:
*   [[../concepts/baum-welch-algorithm.md|Baum-Welch Algorithm]]: For training (learning the model parameters).
*   [[../concepts/forward-algorithm.md|Forward Algorithm]]: For computing the probability of an observed sequence and posterior probabilities of states.
*   [[../concepts/viterbi-algorithm.md|Viterbi Algorithm]]: For finding the most likely sequence of hidden states.

A specific type of HMM used in the near-expiry options model is the [[../concepts/gaussian-hmm.md|Gaussian HMM]].
