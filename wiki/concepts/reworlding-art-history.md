# Reworlding Art History

## Definition
"Reworlding Art History" is a theoretical concept primarily developed by Michelle Antoinette in her 2014 book of the same name. It refers to a critical approach that challenges the Euro-American-centric nature of art markets, art worlds, and art histories.

## Key Aspects
*   **Perspective Shift**: It implies a formulation of the world from particular, often non-Euro-American, perspectives. It registers a "distinct shift in thinking about the world from Euro-American models and practices of world-making to non-Euro-American perspectives."
*   **Disruption of Hegemony**: "Reworlding" is presented as a "tactic" to move beyond dominant, often binary, approaches to non-Euro-American art. It aims to disrupt both homogenizing and essentializing narratives originating from Euro-America and those that might emerge within specific regions like Southeast Asia.
*   **Global Context**: The concept acknowledges the widespread recognition of mutually imbricated processes of localization and globalization, and the agency of artists in negotiating these forces.
*   **Beyond Culturalism**: It critiques approaches that reduce art objects to "mirrors of Southeast Asian cultures" or commodify the "exotic nature of 'the other'." Instead, it seeks to understand art in its specific contexts while also recognizing broader connections and shared human experiences.
*   **Focus on the Contemporary**: The concept is particularly relevant to contemporary art, which, due to its heterogeneity, resists singular aesthetic, ideological, political, geographical, or social frameworks. The 1990s are often cited as a watershed moment when external art institutions began to recognize and engage with "Other" and "peripheral" art worlds.

## Application
Antoinette applies "reworlding" to contemporary Southeast Asian art after 1990, using it as a case study to demonstrate how art from this region can illuminate global processes of "world making" and challenge established art historical canons.
