# Strategic Amnesia of Digital Culture

The concept of "[[Strategic Amnesia of Digital Culture]]" refers to a tendency to forget or overlook past digital practices and histories, often leading to the perception of new technologies and art forms as perpetually "new" or novel. This amnesia, as posited by [[Jussi Parikka]], is particularly characteristic of digital culture.

In the context of Singapore, "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023) argues that this strategic amnesia is essential to the cyclical instrumentalization of technology. Technological developments are frequently framed as being made possible through technocratic masterplanning (e.g., [[IT2000 Masterplan]], [[Smart Nation]]), and the perceived eternal "newness" of [[New Media Art]] technologies justifies continuous attention and investment for national economic benefit.

This amnesia contributes to an ahistorical approach to [[New Media Art]] in Singapore, where early practices like those of [[Lin Hsin Hsin]] and [[tsunamii.net]] are often overlooked or seen as discontinuous fragments. The essay uses [[Media Archaeology]] to disturb this amnesia, aiming to uncover and connect these earlier histories.

## Notes
The concept of [[Strategic Amnesia of Digital Culture]] is critical for understanding why certain histories of technology and art remain unacknowledged, particularly in contexts driven by rapid technological development and nationalistic narratives.
