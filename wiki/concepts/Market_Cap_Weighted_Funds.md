# Market-Cap-Weighted Funds

A market-cap-weighted fund is an investment fund (such as an ETF or mutual fund) where the proportion of each stock in the portfolio is determined by its total market capitalization. Companies with larger market capitalizations (share price multiplied by the number of outstanding shares) will constitute a larger percentage of the fund's holdings.

## Implications

*   **Performance Driven by Large Caps:** The fund's performance is heavily influenced by the movements of its largest constituents.
*   **Sector Concentration:** In sector-specific funds, rapid growth in a few large companies can lead to significant concentration, as seen with [[../entities/Eli_Lilly.md|Eli Lilly]] in the [[../entities/XLV.md|XLV ETF]] due to the success of [[../concepts/GLP-1_Receptor_Agonists.md|GLP-1 receptor agonists]].
*   **Passive Management:** This weighting scheme is common in passive index funds, aiming to replicate the performance of a specific market index.
