# High-Acuity Innovation (Medical Devices)

High-Acuity Innovation is a strategic vector adopted by medical device companies to mitigate the risk of "commoditized" devices being replaced by [[../concepts/glp-1s.md|GLP-1]] drugs. This strategy involves focusing research and development efforts on high-value, complex interventions where pharmacological therapy alone is insufficient to address advanced disease states.

Key examples of this strategy include:

*   **Cardiovascular Interventions:** Companies like [[../entities/boston-scientific.md|Boston Scientific]] and [[../entities/medtronic.md|Medtronic]] recognize that while GLP-1s can reduce systemic cardiovascular risk, they do not resolve structural heart issues such as severe valve stenosis or complex calcified arterial blockages. Therefore, R&D is shifting towards advanced technologies like [[../concepts/intravascular-lithotripsy-ivl.md|Intravascular Lithotripsy (IVL)]] and other structural heart devices. These innovations target the "remaining" patient pool—those with complex, advanced disease that necessitates mechanical intervention regardless of their metabolic state or GLP-1 usage.

This focus allows device makers to differentiate their offerings and maintain relevance in a healthcare landscape increasingly influenced by powerful new pharmacotherapies. It is a core component of the overall [[../concepts/medical-device-response-to-glp-1s.md|medical device response to GLP-1s]].
