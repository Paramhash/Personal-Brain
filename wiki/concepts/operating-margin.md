# Operating Margin

Operating margin is a key financial metric that indicates how much profit a company makes from its core operations, relative to its revenue. It is calculated as operating income divided by revenue, expressed as a percentage.

## Significance
A higher operating margin generally indicates more efficient management and stronger profitability from a company's primary business activities, before accounting for interest and taxes.

## Industry Comparisons
In the medical technology sector, operating margins can vary significantly based on business model and market position:
*   **[[../entities/intuitive-surgical.md|Intuitive Surgical]]**: As a pure-play monopoly with a [[../concepts/razor-and-blade-business-model.md|razor-and-blade business model]], it boasts elite operating margins of ~30% to 33%.
*   **[[../entities/medtronic.md|Medtronic]]**, [[../entities/boston-scientific.md|Boston Scientific]], [[../entities/johnson-and-johnson.md|Johnson & Johnson]] (consolidated): Typically in the 25% to 27% range.
*   **[[../entities/abbott-laboratories.md|Abbott Laboratories]]**: ~22% to 24%, reflecting its diversified portfolio.

Understanding operating margins is crucial for assessing a company's financial health and competitive standing, as highlighted in [[../research/medtronics-competitive-landscape.md|Medtronic's Competitive Landscape]].
