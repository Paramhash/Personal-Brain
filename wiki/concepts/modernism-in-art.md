# Modernism in Art

[[Modernism in Art]] refers to a broad movement in Western art history, typically from the late 19th to mid-20th century, characterized by a break from traditional forms and an emphasis on self-referentiality and the medium itself.

In "[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023), [[Alexander R. Galloway]]'s distinction between working "on" the digital and working "within" the digital is used to characterize artistic approaches. Working "on" the digital, where attention is directed from the outside in, taking the medium itself as its object, is described as an essentially "modernist gesture." This aligns with Clement Greenberg's idea of modernist painting foregrounding the "ineluctable flatness" of the canvas.

The article applies this distinction to [[Lin Hsin Hsin]]'s work, particularly [[Ocarina (1994)]], which is characterized as "modernist in character." Her focus on the digital medium's material properties (e.g., RGB color system, dpi resolution) and her efforts to legitimize digital objects within museological conventions are seen as self-referential acts towards the medium itself. This is contrasted with [[tsunamii.net]]'s [[alpha 3.4 (2002)]], which is described as "contemporary" for working "within" the digital to address broader contexts.

## Notes
The application of "modernist" and "contemporary" labels to digital art practices helps to categorize different approaches to the medium, but the article also cautions against a monolithic, Western-centric understanding of modernism in non-Western contexts like Singapore.
