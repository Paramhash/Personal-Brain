# New Media Art

[[New Media Art]] broadly encompasses artistic practices engaged with technology, spanning categories such as [[Cyberarts]], digital art, [[Internet Art]], computer art, or mixed media practices that utilize or engage with the "technological." The term itself is acknowledged as "slippery" and problematic, often imbued with assumptions about "newness" and ahistoricity.

In Singapore, the early 2000s saw a surge in [[New Media Art]] activity, marked by exhibitions like *Cyberarts: Intersections of Art and Technology* (2001) and institutional support such as the National Arts Council's New Media Arts Fund. However, the field has often been perceived in discontinuous fragments, leading to a "strategic amnesia" about its history.

"[[Excavating Amnesia: A Media Archaeology of Early Internet Art from Singapore]]" (2023) uses [[Media Archaeology]] to challenge this amnesia, examining early instances of [[New Media Art]] in Singapore, including [[Ocarina (1994)]] by [[Lin Hsin Hsin]] and [[alpha 3.4 (2002)]] by [[tsunamii.net]]. The article highlights how the discourse around [[New Media Art]] in Singapore is interwoven with national technological masterplans (e.g., [[IT2000 Masterplan]], [[Smart Nation]]) and regional art historiographies. It also notes the ongoing perception of [[New Media Art]] as a "novelty" even in later years, and its precarious reliance on institutional support.
